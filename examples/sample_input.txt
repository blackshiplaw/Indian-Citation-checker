In the landmark judgment of Kesavananda Bharati Sripadagalvaru v. State of Kerala, (1973) 4 SCC 225, also cited as AIR 1973 SC 1461 and [1973] 4 SCR 541, a 13-judge Constitution Bench held that Parliament cannot alter the basic structure of the Constitution.

Subsequently, in Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India, (1978) 1 SCC 248, the Supreme Court ruled that procedure established by law under Article 21 must be just, fair and reasonable.

This jurisprudence culminated in Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India, (2017) 10 SCC 1, affirming privacy as a fundamental right.

For internet freedom and intermediary speech protections, see Shreya Singhal v. Union of India, (2015) 5 SCC 1.
For consensual adult rights and constitutional morality, see Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India, (2018) 10 SCC 1.
In Delhi High Court jurisprudence, refer to Christian Louboutin SAS v. Nakul Bajaj, 2018 SCC OnLine Del 12215.
